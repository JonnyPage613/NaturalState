Engineering support & Siteworks partnership

What I’m proposing

I want to help you launch and grow Natural State Engineering while we build a separate Siteworks business together. I would bring the website, customer intake, advertising, field coordination and day-to-day operating work. You would bring the engineering services, initial equipment access and agreed startup support.

Your Engineering business would remain entirely yours. This proposal separates how I would be paid for your Engineering assignments from how we would operate and share profits in Siteworks.

Launch your Engineering business

You would retain 100% ownership of Natural State Engineering and lead the professional evaluation, design and permitting work. I would build the customer-facing side of the business and handle agreed excavation and site-support assignments around your professional schedule.

My first digital priorities would be a clear website, useful service pages, online requests, booking and callback options, advertising and organized job records. The field app would let you, me and your installer partner see the work assigned to each person and the next action on every job.

The complete system guide explains my responsibilities, the software, tracker choices, customer notifications and the connections still required for production.

Assignment scope and proposed rates

$250 covers the first four hours of the initial assignment. Additional authorized time is $45 per hour. Time includes loading and securing your equipment, driving, unloading, instructed test-pit excavation, routine flagging and photographs, backfill, cleanup and returning the equipment.

Additional measurements, well-location records, elevation assistance or layout staking would count toward the same recorded time when you request that work. You would direct the technical scope and verify the professional findings.

A separate equipment return for backfilling would add $150. That visit is a separate flat-fee assignment; I would not also bill its backfill time at $45/hour. Additional work outside the return-backfill scope would be agreed before it begins.

Your business would supply the truck and equipment and pay job fuel, approved supplies and any authorized helper. Before dispatch, we would agree on the work and expected time. The customer’s accepted price would not automatically increase because our schedules require a return.

Labor, rental and distribution

For Siteworks jobs, I propose $45/hour for either of us performing necessary work. Siteworks would rent your equipment at $45 per operating hour, capped at $200 per day. If you also work on the job, your labor is paid separately at $45/hour.

A general helper’s proposed base wage is $18/hour; a hired excavator operator’s proposed base wage is $26/hour. Employer taxes, insurance and payroll costs are additional. The calculator includes an editable employer-cost allowance for planning; actual costs would replace it.

Equipment operating hours by day

Siteworks would pay job fuel separately; ordinary equipment maintenance and wear would be covered by the rental rate. Rental time follows actual equipment operation and necessary machine setup—not every hour we are on the property. Shared hauling costs are allocated once. If Siteworks later buys its own machine, the rental income stays with Siteworks.

The daily rental cap applies separately to each rental day. After labor, equipment, job expenses and agreed reserves, the remaining distributable profit would be split 60% to me and 40% to you. Rental receipts cover equipment expenses as well as any return to the equipment owner.

Brand, marketing and contracting

I would start with a professional identity, a clear customer website and convenient ways to request work, book a visit or ask for a callback. I would then create the advertisements, establish the agreed online campaigns and local business listings, and track which efforts produce qualified inquiries and completed jobs.

My monthly digital-service fee includes the agreed advertising setup, ad copy and graphics, campaign monitoring and performance review. Your Engineering business or our Siteworks company would pay its own advertising spend, printing and placement costs. Google, Facebook and Instagram are initial channels to consider, with Bing, Nextdoor, builder/realtor relationships and local publications as appropriate.
