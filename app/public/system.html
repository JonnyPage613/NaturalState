03 · What we would track

Field evidence, travel and business records

Property and engineering evidence

Confirmed parcel reference, service address, site access, feature coordinates and accuracy/source, photographs, notes, measured elevations, soil records, design revisions and submission evidence.

Public parcels and mapped soils provide context. Phone/map positions are field observations, not surveyed boundaries or automatic engineering determinations.

People, vehicles and costs

Assigned company/crew/vehicle, work-stage times, trip start/end, position freshness, distance source, reviewed business purpose, labor hours, machine-hour readings, receipts and invoice references.

A truck tracker does not measure excavator operating hours. I would keep machine-hour-meter readings or approved operator time records separate from truck travel and staff hours.

For a submitted permit packet, the production archive would keep the actual file bytes and versions, not just a status checkbox. Closeout would check that the required records are present on the server. Your official submission would still take place through the state portal, with its confirmation saved back into the job.

Cellular data is a separate operating expense

Hologram currently publishes $1 per active SIM/month plus $0.03/MB, with a $3 SIM charge. As an arithmetic example, 100 MB would cost $4/month before tax and other applicable charges. We would measure actual tracker usage and carrier coverage during the pilot.

1NCE is another option to compare. Its 500 MB lifetime-plan allowance is a total data pool, not 500 MB every month; frequent live updates and reconnects consume it. A ten-year term does not guarantee ten years of continuous fleet tracking without top-ups.

05 · How customer updates work

An assigned trip triggers the message

1 · Assign

Connect the active job to its driver, vehicle and customer contact.

2 · Depart

The driver starts the trip. Tested geofence rules can later confirm departure automatically.

3 · Calculate

A fresh location and road-routing estimate produce the arrival window.

4 · Notify

Send the on-the-way update, retain delivery status and refresh the visit information.

A yard exit by itself does not identify the destination. Automated departure would require an active assigned job and the correct vehicle, with movement/departure rules tested on real trips. A trip to fuel up should not notify an unrelated customer.

The pilot would test driving reports around every 30–60 seconds, then adjust for coverage, data usage and battery needs. A planned two-minute freshness limit would label old positions and suspend a live ETA until a fresh report arrives. Delayed reports become history; duplicate or old events must not send repeat departure messages.

GPS supplies position; a road-routing service supplies the travel estimate. The arrival window changes with stops and traffic where routing supports it. Customers would receive departure, material delay and arrival updates rather than a text for every position report.

Customer tracking, if enabled, would be a private link for the assigned visit and end when that visit closes. On-duty phone tracking would be enabled deliberately; the public website would not expose the fleet.

MapLibre, PostGIS and self-hosted Traccar do not require Supabase Pro or MapTiler subscriptions. Paid hosting, cellular service, routing, messaging, voice and storage choices would be visible in the operating budget.
