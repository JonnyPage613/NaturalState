PROPOSED SERVICE · NOT CONNECTED

24/7 AI receptionist and voice booking

Customers could call the business number or talk directly on the website to understand the services, arrange an on-site evaluation or request a callback. The online form remains available for customers who prefer it. Voice booking would collect the same essential details through a conversation.

One receptionist, two ways to talk

Call the business number: the website opens the phone’s dialer, and the AI answers the call.

Talk on the website: the customer permits microphone access and speaks through their phone or computer’s browser.

Both would use the same approved service information, scheduling rules and customer-record connections. The greeting identifies it as Natural State Engineering’s AI receptionist.

PROPOSED MONTHLY OPERATING BUDGET$80–$100

Budget for the AI voice and telephone service at initial call volume—not a customer service fee or an unlimited-call guarantee.

Plan initially around 100 customer conversations per month; actual cost depends on call length, provider and options. If volume approaches 500 calls, review usage and adjust the budget.

Setup: Jonny would configure and connect the service with AI development assistance. Hiring a setup contractor is not required. Existing Hostinger hosting stays in place; this approach does not require a VPS.

What happens during and after a conversation

  1. Identify the service. Explain soil evaluations / perc-test requests, septic design and permitting support, preliminary property planning or plan review using Tylor-approved descriptions. Ask what the customer was told they need, their property location and deadline.
  2. Find or create the request. A website call can securely use the request the customer just submitted. A separate telephone caller provides and verifies their details before an existing record is used. New customers can provide their details by voice.
  3. Check actual availability. Offer only released appointments that fit the service, travel time and crew capacity. A request for work within 48 hours is treated as a deadline to check, not a guaranteed response time.
  4. Reserve and confirm. Read back the service, property address, access / meeting instructions and chosen time. Confirm a booking only after the scheduling system successfully reserves it. If scope or access still needs review, explain that it is a request awaiting confirmation.
  5. Offer a human callback. Ask whether the customer needs an engineer or any available team member, record the question, and offer an approved callback window. The initial proposal is up to three callbacks a day in selected evening windows, such as 5–6 p.m. Central Time.
  6. Update the team. Save the request and conversation summary to the job workspace, create the map / scheduling handoff from the verified location, and send the team an email alert. The evening callback queue shows who needs a response, what they asked and the promised window. Mark completed callbacks so they are not missed or repeated.

Connections to build

  • Hosted voice provider and business number or call forwarding.
  • Website voice button, microphone permission and secure session handling.
  • Approved service reference material and conversation instructions; no custom model training required for the initial setup.
  • Customer intake and job records, verified property lookup, and calendar availability / reservation tools.
  • Email confirmations, team alerts and the dashboard callback queue. Text-message confirmations are optional and may add provider charges.

Provider and launch plan

Preferred candidate: Retell. Its browser voice and telephone options fit a shared receptionist connected to the custom website. Published voice rates are usage-based; the $80–$100 figure here is our planning allowance.

Packaged alternative: Goodcall. Its Starter plan lists $79/month for 100 unique customers, with overage charges. That means distinct customer phone numbers, not an allowance of exactly 100 calls. Confirm integration fit before selecting it.

Choose the provider, approve services and callback hours, connect records and scheduling, then test new callers, repeat callers, full calendars, changed addresses, failed bookings and notifications before public activation. If a connection fails, collect a callback request rather than inventing a confirmation.
